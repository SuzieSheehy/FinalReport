\chapter{Conclusion}\label{ch:conclusion}

Electron orbital behaviour within both atomic and cyclic-molecular systems was investigated using the Hückel and Hartree-Fock approximations. When compared with experimental results \cite{szwacki_aromatic_2009}\cite{neto_electronic_2009}, the Hückel method produced accurate electronic wavefunctions for both Benzene molecules and three differently-sized Graphene crystals, but corresponding eigenvalues were not confirmed to be accurate. The Hartree-Fock algorithm \cite{laaksonen_fully_1986}\cite{quiney_art_nodate} produced accurate $s$-type Hydrogen energies and wavefunctions in both analytical and numerical forms, but required more computationally-expensive domain resolutions for accurate fully-numerical orbitals, suggesting utility only in systems without analytic alternatives. In comparing $s$-type Helium orbitals, an error was found in the algorithm used for the Hartree-Fock potential $G^{(HF)}$, and therefore, molecular Hartree-Fock results for both Benzene and Graphene could not be properly compared to those from the Hückel method.
